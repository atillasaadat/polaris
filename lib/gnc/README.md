# `lib/gnc/` — GNC Algorithms

Estimation, guidance, and control math (design doc §8), shared by the FSW and
the analysis side. Everything here is pure math over the canonical
`EstimatedState` and frame-tagged vectors — no F´ types, no I/O, no globals —
so the F´ components that ship these algorithms are thin wrappers and the same
code is testable off-target.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `triad.{hpp,cpp}` | TRIAD deterministic two-vector attitude initializer + Shuster's covariance of the solution [black1964][markley2014][shuster1981]; the covariance is also exposed on its own (`triadCovariance`), since it is linear in the two variances and callers split their error budget across it; REQ-ADET-003 |
| `davenport.{hpp,cpp}` | Davenport's q-method: the optimal attitude from N weighted vector pairs (Wahba's problem via the 4×4 K matrix) with the inverse-Fisher covariance, gated on observability in both frames [davenport1968][markley2014][shuster1981]; MEKF cold start/re-init only; REQ-ADET-003 |
| `coarse_attitude.{hpp,cpp}` | Coarse SS+MAG+IMU attitude estimator: gyro propagation, TRIAD acquisition/update, fixed-gain complementary blend over a systematic covariance floor, eclipse coasting with a validity horizon [markley2014][wertz1978]; REQ-ADET-002 |
| `mekf.{hpp,cpp}` | 6-state multiplicative EKF on `[δθ; δb]`: closed-form propagation with the exact `Φ₁₂`, Farrenkopf discrete `Qd`, vector measurements one at a time with per-update NIS gating, Joseph-form update, exact multiplicative reset [lefferts1982][markley2014][farrenkopf1978][barshalom2001]; REQ-ADET-001 |
| `orbit_od.{hpp,cpp}` | **GNSS-aided orbit determination**, the §8.3 half of `EstimatedState`: a 6-state EKF on ECI `[δr; δv]` over a coarse force model — an **8×8 EGM2008 truncation** (`geopotential.hpp`) + a single-band exponential-density atmosphere [tapley2004][montenbruck2000][vallado2013]. The fidelity gap to the truth sim's 200×200 stack is the design, not an omission. It originally flew closed-form two-body + J2 on the argument that GNSS dominates the solution; *that argument holds for the estimate and is unchanged*, but it never covered the two things the dynamics is load-bearing for — coasting an outage and onboard ephemeris prediction — where the J2 truncation was the whole budget (3.17 m over the 300 s horizon against a 32×32 truth, 14.3 m over 699 s against GMAT). What survives is the third argument, and it still does the work: the remaining truncation is absorbed as process noise **sized from its own measurement** (`q_a = 3·δr(T)²/T³` against a measured **1.28 m** over the horizon), so improving the field re-derives `q_a` rather than leaving a now-conservative value in place. Degree 0 restores two-body and `zonal_j2` the closed-form J2 — how the golden test matches GMAT's fidelity — and the two paths are mutually exclusive by config validation. The field is evaluated in **ECEF, per integration sub-step**: a zonal term needs only the pole, but a tesseral is fixed to the rotating Earth and holding one rotation across a 60 s step would smear it by 0.25° of longitude. `Φ = I + Fh + ½F²h²` on a **numerical** Jacobian, deliberately, so it cannot drift from the force model it differentiates (pinned against the closed-form two-body gravity gradient); CWNA `Q` with its cross-terms. Ingest owns the §3.2/§6.2 conversion — `TAI = GPS + 19 s` and a full ECEF→ECI **state** transform (REQ-CONV-001) — and refuses a fix the uploaded EOP table cannot cover rather than extrapolating. **Fix latency is corrected on the measurement, not the filter**: a fix tagged behind the filter's epoch (the normal case, since the GNC cycle has propagated since it was measured) has its PVT advanced on the receiver's *own reported velocity* plus the onboard acceleration, `r += vτ + ½aτ²`, with `R` inflated by `(σ_v τ)²`. At 7.6 km/s an uncorrected 50 ms latency is 380 m of along-track error, an order above the receiver's own noise; the residual after correction is `O(τ³·jerk)`, micrometres — the benign corner of Bar-Shalom §5.6, where the measurement determines the whole state and carries its own velocity [barshalom2001][kim2025]. Bounded by `max_fix_latency_s`; a latent fix *without* velocity is refused rather than advanced on the prior, since borrowing the filter's velocity folds its error into a measurement required to be independent of it. Position and velocity fold in as two sequential Joseph-form 3-row updates, each χ²₃-gated, with `R` built from the receiver's **own per-fix anisotropic** σ rotated into ECI rather than flattened to a scalar. Past the coast horizon the solution is declared invalid **and dropped**, so the next fix re-acquires whole (the coarse estimator's discipline, not the MEKF's — there is no converged bias here worth keeping, and past the horizon the model error is a systematic a stale prior would only drag the fresh fix toward). Validated two ways that are not interchangeable: GMAT at **matched fidelity** (3.5 mm two-body, 1.0 cm J2, 1.2 cm at 8×8 — `tests/golden/orbit_od_golden_test.cpp`), and NEES/NIS Monte Carlo for covariance consistency (6.03 against 6, 3.01 against 3) [barshalom2001] |
| `geopotential.{hpp,cpp}` | **The onboard low-degree geopotential**, over the compiled-in `egm2008_low_degree.hpp` table: the unnormalized **Cunningham V/W** recursion [cunningham1970][montenbruck2000], ~60 lines, `(N+2)²` doubles of stack scratch, no precomputed tables and no Legendre evaluation — the auxiliary `V_nm + iW_nm` are built by pure recursion from `V₀₀ = Rₑ/r`, so there is no `1/cos φ` and the poles are not special, and the acceleration falls out of the same table one degree higher. This is a **third** implementation of a field `sim/world/gravity_field` already evaluates two ways, and the header says why: the sim's carries `std::vector` coefficient and normalization tables sized at construction and a runtime-settable degree — all correct for a 200×200 truth model, all disallowed on the flight path (§3.6). Made safe by cross-validation rather than by reuse: `tests/unit/geopotential_test.cpp` pins it against the sim's normalized-Gottlieb engine at **every degree 2..8** over a position spread including both poles (worst 5e-9 relative), and the golden test pins it against **GMAT's EGM96** at matched degree (1.2 cm over 699 s). Degree 0 is *exactly* point-mass and degree 2 order 0 *exactly* the textbook closed-form J2, both to machine precision, which is what lets `orbit_od`'s analytic tests keep testing the closed forms. V/W is safe here and not at 200 because its sectoral terms grow like `(2n−1)!!`; `tools/gravity/cxxtable.py` refuses to emit a table above degree 20 so the evaluator can never be handed coefficients it cannot carry. Returns zero — never a NaN — for a sub-surface or non-finite position, and clamps degree/order rather than reading past the array |
| `egm2008_low_degree.hpp` | **Generated, committed, do not edit.** The EGM2008 coefficients truncated to degree/order 8 and **de-normalized**, emitted by `tools/gravity/cxxtable.py` from the committed ICGEM `.gfc` (`tests/golden/EGM2008_to200.gfc`, the reference datum per §3.7 — this is a derivation *from* it, not a substitute for it) [pavlis2012][icgemformat]. 45 coefficient pairs, ~1 kB of `.rodata`; compiled in rather than uploaded, so there is no validity, staleness or CRC story. De-normalization happens on the ground because the V/W recursion consumes unnormalized coefficients and doing it in flight would be arithmetic repeated every cycle for a constant. `kGm` and `kReferenceRadius` are the **model's own** header values, not WGS84's, and travel with the table because the coefficients were solved with them — `OrbitOdConfig::isValid` refuses a config that disagrees. `tests/tools/test_gravity_cxxtable.py` regenerates and compares the coefficients every run (numbers, not bytes: clang-format owns the file's whitespace), so the derivation cannot drift from its source |
| `albedo_correction.{hpp,cpp}` | Closed-form inverse of the §6.2 Earth-albedo pull on a measured sun vector: the modelled directed offset toward the sunlit Earth rotated back out, credited only the fraction of itself the centroid model can honestly claim (`albedo_dispersion_fraction`, §19.2) [bhanderi2005][wertz1978][markley2014]; refuses — leaving the measurement untouched, for the caller to weight at the *uncorrected* σ — on missing or degenerate geometry rather than correcting on an invented nadir; `tests/unit/albedo_correction_test.cpp` sweeps the geometry |
| `unit_voting.{hpp,cpp}` | The **shared** redundancy-vote policy (§8.2), with no idea what it is voting on: per-unit plausibility ladder, per-axis **median** at ≥ 3 units — never an average, whose breakdown point is zero [rousseeuw1987][gilmore1972] — pairwise detection at exactly 2 with identification against a caller-supplied reference behind margin and confirmation gates, and a latched exclusion re-admitted on **the criterion that excluded it**. Everything physical is the caller's, because that is the only part that differs between sensor types; a third redundant sensor is another wrapper, not another copy of this state machine |
| `imu_voting.{hpp,cpp}` | The gyro half: `unit_voting` plus a physical **body-rate** limit (not the gyro's measurement range — that is what a railed unit reports) and the MEKF's propagated rate as the identification reference, itself range-checked so an upstream fault cannot latch out the healthy unit. The reference vehicle's **two** IMUs put it on the pairwise rung, which reports no rate at all when the filter's rate is unavailable; REQ-ADET-008, REQ-ADET-009 |
| `mag_voting.{hpp,cpp}` | The magnetometer half: `unit_voting` plus the **IGRF-magnitude band** — the natural physical gate, attitude-free and tracking the field from ~22 to ~52 µT over an orbit where a fixed full-scale check would admit everything below saturation — and the modelled field rotated into body axes as the identification reference, gated on the attitude's *quality* rather than its validity flag. An unattributable disagreement costs the magnetic pair, not the body rate; REQ-ADET-011 |
| `st_alignment.{hpp,cpp}` | Commanded inter-star-tracker alignment (§8.2): the **king** tracker's mounting defines the body frame, and every other unit's constant rotation relative to it is estimated as the maximum-eigenvalue quaternion average of simultaneous solution pairs [markley2007] — a 4×4 accumulator, so O(1) in the window length, with the RMS residual and the consistency eigen-gap falling out of the same eigenvalues exactly. Attitude pairs have no degenerate geometry, so the eigen-gap gate detects a *unit fault* rather than a bad geometry, and there is no coverage gate; REQ-ADET-013 |
| `bdot.{hpp,cpp}` | B-dot magnetic detumble: the field-strength-normalised law `m = -(k/‖B‖²)Ḃ` with Avanzini & Giulietti's gain floor [avanzini2012][markley2014], the derivative formed from **successive quiet-window magnetometer samples and their own time tags** (never the control period, because the §7 interlock does not deliver a usable sample every cycle), the demand divided by the duty factor so the *average* dipole is what the gain asked for, and per-rod clamping — componentwise, which preserves the sign of every term in the energy rate and so keeps a saturated B-dot monotonically dissipative. Ships the rate entry/exit hysteresis the mode manager will read; REQ-ACTL-001 |
| `attitude_pid.{hpp,cpp}` | Quaternion-error PID: `δθ = 2·sgn(δq₀)·vec(q_ref ⊗ q̂⁻¹)` short-way-round, rate-error damping, integrator clamped **and** frozen while the torque is saturated, torque saturation by a single scale so an over-demand is a slower correction about the right axis rather than a faster one about the wrong axis [wie1989][markley2014][astrom2008]; the reported error angle is the `atan2` form; REQ-ACTL-002 |
| `rw_allocation.{hpp,cpp}` | Body torque → per-wheel torque across the N-wheel array: L2 minimum-norm from a pseudo-inverse factorised once at construction, and exact **L-∞** (min–max) for a four-wheel array by minimising the convex piecewise-linear `max\|u_p + αn\|` over the one-dimensional null space, with `α = 0` in the candidate set so L-∞ is never worse than L2 [markley2014][wie2008]. Saturation scales the whole vector, preserving the commanded direction. Null spaces of dimension > 1 (five or more wheels) are an LP this module refuses rather than approximates; REQ-ACTL-002 |
| `rw_friction.{hpp,cpp}` | Drive-level wheel **friction feedforward** (§8.5): adds `-tau_f` per wheel *after* the allocation and *before* the drive command, so the net rotor torque is the one the allocation asked for [armstrong1994]. The Coulomb term's `sgn(omega)` runs through a bounded linear blend over a configured deadband speed [karnopp1985] — Karnopp's zero-velocity band in feedforward form, whose cost is stated rather than hidden: inside the band the friction is deliberately under-compensated, which is the honest price of not chattering at a wheel's zero crossing. The viscous term needs no blend. Never over-compensates at a trim `k <= 1` (`|c| <= |tau_f|` exactly), and the torque-box clamp is applied so that what it removes is the *compensation* and never the control demand. A wheel with no usable tachometer has no speed sign, so it is passed through uncompensated rather than given a guessed one. **The per-wheel trim is a real closed-loop knob, not just a safety margin**: the friction it removes was also damping the stored momentum, and on the reference vehicle `k = 0.5` beats `k = 1.0` on both a loaded and a near-empty array (2.77 -> 1.38 deg and 0.12 -> 0.25 deg, against 1.52 / 1.09 at full compensation — measured on the 0.5 N·m·s wheels the vehicle flew through Push 59; the Push 60 re-sized wheel's friction is an order of magnitude smaller and the sweep has not been re-flown there); REQ-ACTL-010 |
| `momentum.{hpp,cpp}` | Wheel-array momentum accounting and magnetic desaturation (§8.5): stored momentum `h = W(I_w ω_w)` from the tachometers — refused whole when any wheel reports no usable speed, because a missing term in a sum is an unknown vector of that wheel's full magnitude — the desaturation predicate with entry/exit hysteresis (reusing `bdot`'s magnitude state machine rather than re-implementing it), the §9 envelope flag, and the cross-product unloading law `m = k_d(Δh × B)/‖B‖²` [camillo1980][markley2014][wie2008], field-normalised and duty-divided for the same reasons B-dot is, returned unclamped so the caller clamps once per rod — which stays dissipative term by term because componentwise clamping preserves each component's sign in an orthonormal rod basis; REQ-ACTL-009, REQ-ACTL-010 |
| `disturbance.{hpp,cpp}` | Onboard disturbance feedforward, §8.5 tiers 1–2: the modelled gravity-gradient `3n²(n̂ × J n̂)` [hughes1986][wertz1978] and residual-dipole `m_res × B` torques on *estimated* states (free functions — nothing is fitted, and a cycle missing an input simply does not get that term), plus the momentum-based residual-torque observer, which differences the total system momentum `H = Jω + h_w` [wie2008], subtracts the modelled part and low-passes what is left. Internal torques cancel identically in `H`, so what it sees is external by construction. **One estimator, two consumers**: the same estimate is fed forward and gated as the §9 momentum-anomaly monitor, with a persistence count both ways; REQ-ACTL-011 |
| `dipole_estimation.{hpp,cpp}` | §8.5 **tier 3**, attitude side: fits the body-fixed residual dipole `m` from the tier-2 observer's unmodelled-torque estimate, replacing the configured magnetic-cleanliness *allocation* with a measurement [inamori2011]. `tau = m x B = -[Bx]m` is linear in `m`, so this is information-form RLS with exponential forgetting in **time** [ljung1999] — an O(1) 3x3 accumulator. `-[Bx]` is **rank 2 at every instant** (the component of `m` along `B` is invisible), so the estimate is gated on two different things and the refusal names which: `λ_min/λ_max` — the same information-matrix ratio the flight Davenport seed and `analysis/control/observability.py` use — says whether the *field has turned*, and `λ_min` itself says whether there is enough *evidence*, readable as an effective sample count and equivalently as a bound on the published sigma. Precision **saturates at the forgetting horizon**, not at the pass length, so the memory time is the ground's only lever on it. The fit absorbs any unmodelled torque correlated with `B` (aero, SRP, friction) as a **bias** no least-squares covariance can see, which is why the protection against a bad fit feeding tier-1 forward is the configured cleanliness bound — outside it the fit is **refused, not clamped**; REQ-ACTL-011 |
| `mag_calibration.{hpp,cpp}` | Attitude-free magnetometer hard/soft-iron calibration: streaming O(1) ten-parameter ellipsoid fit of the raw field against the onboard IGRF **magnitude**, symmetric PD square root for the soft-iron correction, orientation-coverage/conditioning/positive-definiteness/residual-improvement gates [alonso2002complete][alonso2002twostep][vasconcelos2011][higham2008], each refusal naming its gate through the `MagCalibrationRefusal` overload of `solve`; the §8.1 `MAG_CAL_START` flow on `flight::AttitudeEstimator` runs it |

The coarse estimator is the **Safe-mode floor** (§10): with the analytic Sun
ephemeris fallback (§11.3) it needs neither a star tracker nor an uploaded
table, so it can always be run.

Tuning values (`min_sin_angle`, `triad_gain`, the per-source σ split) have **no
in-code defaults** — they are mission configuration (§19.3), and a
default-constructed config deliberately fails validation.

Flight-safe: fixed-size Eigen, no heap, no exceptions, no recursion, bounded
loops, return codes checked, finiteness checks on every published output.
